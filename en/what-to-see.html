What to see in Castel Gandolfo.

10 essential places Verified hours & prices 2026 season

Within a few hundred metres, Castel Gandolfo gathers four hundred years of papal art, two millennia of Roman archaeology and a singular panorama over the crater of Lake Albano. These are the ten essential stops, all walkable in a single day, with hours and prices verified for the 2026 season.

01 · Papal museum

Apostolic Palace of Castel Gandolfo.

The summer residence of the Popes since 1626, open to the public as a museum since 2016. Visitors walk through the papal apartments, the library, the throne room and the private chapel, furnished as the Popes who lived there left them.

Admission €11.00 full price, audio guide included. Combined ticket Palace + Villa Barberini Gardens + Antiquarium €26.00 full price. Organised groups €450 (Palace) and €650 (combined).

02 · Gardens & Borgo Laudato Si'

Villa Barberini, the pontifical gardens.

Fifty-five hectares of extraterritorial greenery, with century-old cypresses, the Bergantino nymphaeum, Roman statuary and the remains of the villa of the emperor Domitian. Since 2023 they have been reorganised as Borgo Laudato Si', the integral-ecology itinerary launched by Francis.

Visits by electric shuttle train (about one hour) or on foot with a guide. Gardens + Antiquarium ticket €26.00 full price, €15.00 reduced. The «Saturday train» from Roma San Pietro €20.00, reduced €14.00.

03 · The town's drawing room

Piazza della Libertà & Bernini.

The heart of Castel Gandolfo, laid out by Bernini: the Apostolic Palace on one side, the collegiate church of San Tommaso da Villanova on the other (designed by Bernini himself in 1660), the baroque fountain at the centre. This is where the Pope recites the summer Angelus.

On weekdays the square is quiet, with historic cafés and small shops; on papal days it fills with the faithful. Do not miss the belvedere behind the square, which opens onto the crater of the lake.

Seven more sights

Between archaeology, art and views.

From the Vatican Observatory to the remains of Domitian's villa, from the Doric nymphaeum to the Roman outlet of the lake: the town holds treasures that classic tours often overlook.

04 · Astronomy

Vatican Observatory (Specola Vaticana)

The papal astronomical observatory with four historic telescopes. Visits by appointment.

05 · Archaeology

Antiquarium of the Pontifical Villas

Sculptures, mosaics and finds from Domitian's villa, over which the papal gardens now stand.

06 · Ancient engineering

Outlet tunnel of Lake Albano

An artificial tunnel dug by the Romans in 398 BC, still working after 2,400 years.

07 · Domitian's villa

Doric & Bergantino nymphaea

Two grotto-sanctuaries of the imperial villa, hidden in the vegetation of the gardens.

08 · Baroque

Collegiate church of San Tommaso da Villanova

The parish church designed by Bernini in 1660, with frescoes by Pietro da Cortona.

09 · Panorama

Belvedere over the crater

A natural balcony over Lake Albano, with views as far as Monte Cavo and the Mediterranean.

10 · Spirituality

Church of the Madonna del Lago

A small sanctuary on the lakeshore, among the most photographed and best loved by locals.

Frequently asked

Before you set off.

How much does admission to the Apostolic Palace cost?
The full-price ticket is €11.00 and includes an audio guide. The combined Palace + Gardens + Antiquarium ticket costs €26.00 full price and €15.00 reduced. Organised groups €450 (Palace only) or €650 (combined).
How long do you need to see everything?
The Apostolic Palace takes 60–90 minutes, the Villa Barberini gardens 45–60 minutes by shuttle train or 90 minutes on foot with a guide. Piazza della Libertà, the collegiate church and the old town take about an hour. In total, a full day.
Is booking required?
Yes, all admissions to the pontifical sites require online booking on the official Vatican Museums website. On Sundays with a papal Angelus the square is free but crowded: arrive early.
Is it accessible for visitors with disabilities?
The Apostolic Palace is partly accessible, with internal lifts. The gardens have accessible shuttle-train routes. The collegiate church has a side ramp. The square is on a slight slope but manageable.
Are there closing days?
The Pontifical Villas close on Sundays and on days with a papal Angelus. Hours change between high and low season. The calendar is published monthly on the official website.

Ready for the visit?

Less than 40 minutes by train from Roma Termini. The combined ticket + Saturday train takes you straight to the pontifical gardens.
