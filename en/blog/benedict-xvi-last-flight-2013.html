28 February 2013 — Benedict XVI's last flight.

October 20268 minute readEditorial team

For three hours, one winter day, Castel Gandolfo was the place where a pontificate came to an end. Benedict XVI arrived by helicopter as Pope and, at eight in the evening, remained there as Pope emeritus. Here is how it happened, hour by hour, according to the official documents of the Holy See.

The announcement of 11 February

On the morning of 11 February 2013 Benedict XVI had called an ordinary consistory in the Vatican for the canonisation of the martyrs of Otranto. At its close, unexpectedly, he announced his resignation (Vatican City State). In the Declaratio, read in Latin (Zenit), he explained that governing the barque of Saint Peter requires “strength of mind and body”, and that this strength had deteriorated in him; the resignation would take effect “as from 28 February 2013, at 20:00 hours”, when the See of Rome would be vacant (Holy See).

The director of the Papal Villas was told in confidence: on the evening of 28 February the Pope would move to Castel Gandolfo while a home was prepared for him in the Mater Ecclesiae monastery in the Vatican (Vatican City State). The town knew Benedict XVI well: he had first come on 5 May 2005, a few days after his election, and had spent every summer there.

11 am, Clementine Hall

The last day began in the Vatican. At 11 am, in the Clementine Hall, the Pope took leave of the cardinals present in Rome. Before shaking hands with each of them, he spoke a sentence that would be long remembered: “among you, in the College of Cardinals, there is also the future pope to whom today I promise my unconditional reverence and obedience” (Holy See).

Just after 5 pm, take-off

In the afternoon Benedict XVI left the Apostolic Palace. According to the Holy See Press Office, “shortly after 5 pm” he left by helicopter from the Vatican heliport for Castel Gandolfo, arriving after a fifteen-minute flight (Holy See Press Office). Reports at the time place the take-off between 5:05 and 5:07 pm, as the bells of Saint Peter's rang (EWTN News). On arrival he was met, among others, by Cardinal Giuseppe Bertello, president of the Governorate, and the mayor of Castel Gandolfo (Inside the Vatican).

5:38 pm, the loggia

The square was packed with the faithful of the diocese of Albano. At 5:38 pm the Pope appeared at the central loggia of the Apostolic Palace (Holy See Press Office) and spoke off the cuff for a few minutes. They were his last public words as Pontiff:

“I am simply a pilgrim beginning the last leg of his pilgrimage on this earth.”
Benedict XVI, loggia of the Apostolic Palace, 28 February 2013

A moment earlier he had said that this day was different from the ones before: he was no longer Supreme Pontiff of the Catholic Church, or rather he would be until eight in the evening and then no longer. He promised to keep working with all his inner strength for the good of the Church and of humanity, gave his blessing and took his leave with a simple “Thank you, good night!” (Holy See). Then he withdrew to his apartment (Vatican City State).

8 pm, the great door

The crowd did not leave. At 8 pm, before a brightly lit and packed square, the great door of the Palace was closed; at the same moment the flag that flew over the Palace whenever the Pope was in residence was lowered. It was the visible sign that the See was vacant (Vatican City State). The resignation also ended the service of the Swiss Guard, the Pope's personal bodyguard: from then on the former Pope's security was in the hands of the Vatican Gendarmerie alone (Wikipedia).

Two months as Pope emeritus

Benedict XVI stayed at Castel Gandolfo until 2 May 2013, when he returned to the Vatican (Vatican City State). In those weeks the conclave elected Cardinal Jorge Mario Bergoglio on 13 March. On 23 March, late in the morning, Pope Francis landed at the heliport of the Papal Villas, where the Pope emeritus was waiting: the two got into the same car and went to the Palace for a conversation and lunch, a historic first according to the Vatican account (Vatican City State).

Today the loggia of that farewell can be seen from Piazza della Libertà, the heart of the town. For anyone who looks at it knowing this story, it is the balcony from which a Pope said good night and chose to become a pilgrim.
