The Apostolic Palace as museum — what you see after 2016.

October 20267 minute readEditorial team

For almost four centuries the main floor of the Palace of Castel Gandolfo was a closed world, reserved for the Pope and his closest staff. Since 22 October 2016 anyone can climb the grand staircase and enter the rooms where the popes received guests, worked, prayed and slept. Here is what you see today, and what has changed with the return of the popes.

A gift in three stages

The opening was gradual. Pope Francis, who unlike his predecessors spent his summers in Rome, opened the gardens to the public in 2014 and the following year part of the Palace as a portrait gallery (CNS / The Boston Pilot). On 21 October 2016 the Vatican Museums presented the last stage: the Papal Apartment, for centuries the popes' most private summer refuge, open to the public from the next day on a route designed and run by the Museums (Vatican Museums). The rooms were opened without structural changes (Wikipedia).

At the inauguration the director of the Vatican Museums, Antonio Paolucci, promised visitors pure beauty: from the great terrace, the blue eye of the lake and the mountains covered in untouched woods, and in the rooms, the murmur of history (Vatican Museums).

First floor: the portraits of the popes

The ticket, sold at the Palace ticket office or on the Vatican Museums website, gives access to the first-floor gallery, with portraits of all the popes from the sixteenth century to the present, and to the second-floor rooms where the popes' daily life took place (Vatican News). The first floor also hosts temporary exhibitions: in February 2024 “Castel Gandolfo 1944” opened, devoted to the months when Pius XII opened the Villas to 12,000-13,000 displaced people and more than forty children were born in the Pope's bed (Vatican News). The audioguide tells five centuries of papal history through paintings, relics, vestments, uniforms and curiosities (Vatican Museums).

Second floor: from the throne to the bedroom

The Apartment route follows the order of court ceremony. It begins in the Hall of the Swiss, where the guard was mounted, and continues through the Throne Room and the Consistory Room, antechambers to the private rooms. Then come the spaces reserved for the Pope's exclusive use: the Library, the private Study and the offices of the private secretary and assistant secretary, where the Pope's personal valet also had his place (Vatican Museums).

“The places where the Pope received, lived, wrote, slept and prayed in his private chapel.”
Barbara Jatta, director of the Vatican Museums, 2024

Two rooms alone are worth the visit. The Pope's bedroom, which in 1944 Pius XII set aside for expectant mothers hidden in the Palace, and the private Chapel, where for the first time two popes, Benedict XVI and the newly elected Francis, prayed together (Vatican Museums). The chapel holds a copy of the Madonna of Częstochowa (Vatican News). Pius XII and Paul VI died in that same bed, and John Paul II recovered there after the 1981 assassination attempt (Religion News Service).

Rooms on request

Some rooms are not usually on the ordinary route but, director Barbara Jatta explained, can be opened on request: the chapel of Urban VIII frescoed by Simone Lagi, the billiard room and the music room (Vatican News). On 17 and 18 May 2026, for International Museum Day, the Vatican Museums exceptionally opened three leisure rooms of the apartment: the Billiard Room, with two paintings by Giovanni Angeloni about Clement XIV, the Television Room and the Music Room, which keeps Paul VI's record player (Vatican Museums). Outside the Palace, the ticket includes the Giardino del Moro and the Secret Garden, overlooking Rome and the lake (Vatican News).

Museum and residence, from 2026

With Leo XIV history took a step back. In summer 2025 the Pope stayed at Villa Barberini, leaving the Palace to the museum; in July 2026 he moved back into the Apostolic Palace (Wikipedia). In spring some outlets had reported that the museum would close for good, but things turned out differently: the museum stayed closed during the Pope's stay, from 5 to 27 July, and reopened after his return to Rome (Rome Reports). Before setting off, check days and hours on the official 2026-2027 calendar of the Papal Palace (Vatican Museums).
