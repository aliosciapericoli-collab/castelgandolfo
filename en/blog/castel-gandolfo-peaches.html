The peaches that charmed the Popes.

July 20268 minute readby the editors

There is a story in Castel Gandolfo that the Popes never left for Rome without taking a full basket with them. They were extraordinarily sweet, yellow or white, with skin as velvet as a cheek. The peaches of the town. Ninety years ago someone decided to give them a festival.

The first Peach Festival (Sagra delle Pesche) of Castel Gandolfo was held in 1936. Those were the years when rural Italy was discovering food festivals as a tool of local identity, and the papal town, which had grown peaches on its volcanic hillsides since the eighteenth century, had more than one good reason. In 2026 the festival celebrates its ninetieth edition, from 20 to 26 July.

The Castel Gandolfo peach

It is not a single variety but a local ecotype. The peaches grown on the volcanic soils around the lake have precise characteristics: freestone flesh that separates easily from the stone, high sugars thanks to the lakeside microclimate, and intense aromas owed to soils rich in potassium and volcanic ash. There are yellow-fleshed varieties — more aromatic, more widespread — and white-fleshed ones, exceptionally sweet and delicate.

Harvesting runs from the second half of June to the first week of August. Peak ripeness coincides, not by chance, with the week of the festival: hence the calendar that has settled over recent decades between 20 and 26 July.

From the pontifical gardens to the table

Peaches are said to have been grown inside the Pontifical Villas as well, in the kitchen gardens that supplied fruit and vegetables to the Palace. Eighteenth-century records mention peach orchards among the gardens of Villa Barberini, where century-old trees can still be seen today.

In the cooking of the Castelli the peach appears at every level: from locally made preserves to trattoria desserts (peaches in Frascati wine, peach cheesecake, sorbets), from summer cocktails (the Castelli bellini, with Frascati Superiore in place of Prosecco) to savoury dishes — grilled peach with goat's cheese and honey is by now a classic of the area's gourmet restaurants.

“It is not a peach. It is the taste of a summer you can only try here.”
A farmer of the Castelli · September 2025

The festival: a week of celebration

The 90th edition of the Peach Festival runs from 20 to 26 July 2026. Seven days of food stalls along the main street, wineries from the Castelli, guided tastings, cooking shows with local chefs and live music every evening. The high point is Saturday's historic parade, with allegorical floats and the blessing of the peaches in Piazza della Libertà.

Free entry, stalls open from 5 pm to midnight. During the festival the old town is closed to traffic. The best approach is to arrive by train from Rome, or park at Lago Nord and walk up.

A symbol that endures

Ninety editions is a rare milestone. The festival has survived the war, the Second Vatican Council, the deaths of Popes, the pandemic. Every July, on cue, the town fills with the scent of ripe peaches. It is a rite that predates mass tourism and fusion cooking. A way, perhaps, of remembering that some things endure. Even the simplest. Even a peach.
