23 November 1820 — the first postbox in the world?

November 20265 minute readEditorial team

You have to look carefully. In Piazza della Libertà, between the Apostolic Palace and Bernini's fountain, on the wall between the Town Hall and the Post Office there is a slot in the marble. A plaque calls it the first letter box in the world. Two hundred and six years ago, in these very days of November, the town council decided to make it.

A postal problem

In 1820 mail for Castel Gandolfo did not reach the village: it stopped in nearby Albano. There letters and parcels waited for a messenger, paid half a baiocco per letter, to come and collect them. But the problem came back on the return trip: the messenger could neither read nor write, and getting the post to the right people was a struggle (Comune di Castel Gandolfo). The baiocco was the everyday coin of the Papal States, to which the village belonged.

The session of 23 November

The solution was put to the priors by councillor Angelo Antonio Iacorossi, also spelled Jacorossi in the records. On 23 November 1820 the Municipal Council approved the project with 11 votes in favour and 3 against; the resolution is now kept at the Historical Museum of Communication, in the Ministry building in Rome's EUR district (Comune di Castel Gandolfo). The plaque placed in 1995, for the 175th anniversary, lists those present at that session: Lieutenant Paolo Mallozzi, chief magistrate Marco Troiani, the priors Candido Marazzi and Filippo Albenzi, and the councillors, Iacorossi among them (Historical Marker Database).

The idea was simple: a slot in the square for posting outgoing letters, collected in one place rather than passed from hand to hand. Two hundred years later, in 2020, the town council added a second plaque, made by La Musa Mosaici, with a QR code that links to the story of the box (Comune di Castel Gandolfo). The original bronze plaque, with its dark raised lettering, is a little hard to read (Italia.it).

“The first mailbox in the world for sending letters.”
The 1995 plaque, Piazza della Libertà

Really the first?

Some caution is needed here. Even Wikipedia, writing about Castel Gandolfo, describes it as the one “that local tradition identifies as the first postbox in the world” (Wikipedia). Other places have older dates: the first post boxes are believed to have been installed in and around Paris as early as 1653, and in Britain a box set into the wall of the Wakefield post office is dated 1809 and is considered the oldest in the country (Wikipedia). Castel Gandolfo's record, in short, is a local point of pride rather than an established fact. None of these dates takes anything away from the village's story: they simply place it in its European context.

What remains certain is just as interesting. The resolution is a dated, preserved document, and it shows a small papal town that, twenty years before the Penny Black, the first adhesive stamp issued in Britain on 1 May 1840, found its own practical answer to a postal problem. At that time, after all, in Britain it was normally the recipient who paid the postage on delivery (Wikipedia).

How to find it

The box is a marble rectangle with a slot, set into the wall between the Town Hall and the post office in Piazza della Libertà; it is a few minutes' walk from Castel Gandolfo station (Italia.it). It is worth stopping, reading the two plaques and then looking up at the square, which has not changed all that much since 1820.

The setting is the very heart of the village. The same square is overlooked by the Papal Palace designed by Carlo Maderno, the pontifical collegiate church of San Tommaso da Villanova and the fountain, both by Bernini for Pope Alexander VII (Comune di Castel Gandolfo). In 1820 the letter box was therefore a thoroughly modern object set in a Baroque stage already a century and a half old: a small administrative innovation in the shadow of the popes' residence. Anyone passing today can pair it with a visit to Bernini's church and the lake viewpoint, a few steps away.
