The Madonna of the Lake — the church wanted by Paul VI.

October 20266 minute readEditorial team

In town everyone calls it the Madonna del Lago, but its name is the church of Maria Santissima del lago Albano. It stands on the shore below the village and can be recognised from afar by its white fan-shaped roof. It is not old: it is a church of the 1970s, born from a Pope's wish and from a festival of the people who worked around the lake.

First the feast, then the church

The story begins in 1954, a Marian Year, when the parish priest of Castel Gandolfo, Don Dino Sella, founded the feast of the Madonna del Lago to involve all the faithful who worked around the lake (Vatican News). At its heart was a procession of boats dedicated to the Madonna, setting out from the CONI facilities and reaching the locality of I Quadri, near the Bergantino nymphaeum (Wikipedia).

On the shore, however, there was no church. Paul VI, who from 1963 spent every summer at Castel Gandolfo, from mid-July to mid-September, decided to have one built. It was one of the works he wanted for the townspeople, together with the papal primary school that now bears his name and the church of San Paolo in the district that grew up along the Appian Way (Vatican City State).

A white sail on the lake

The design was entrusted to Francesco Vacchini, technical director of the Fabbrica di San Pietro; work began in 1966 and ended in 1977. The architect had a precise problem to solve: give comfortable room to at least six hundred people on hot summer days, without making the fifty or so resident faithful feel lost in winter. The answer was to split the church in two: an indoor hall for the cold months and an open-air space for the summer crowds (Wikipedia).

Over everything rises a fan-shaped structure of white-painted reinforced concrete, topped by a cross. For Vacchini it was a metaphor for the sail of the little boat of the Prince of the Apostles sailing on the lake, and also a sign visible from every point of the shore. Inside, much of the liturgical furnishing is by the sculptor Tommaso Gismondi: the Stations of the Cross, the statue of the Madonna, the tabernacle and a Pietà donated by Paul VI (Wikipedia).

“It was the fulfilment of a deep wish of his, born of his pastoral concern.”
John Paul II, homily at the Madonna del Lago, 2 September 1979

15 August 1977

Paul VI consecrated the church on 15 August 1977, the feast of the Assumption (Comune di Castel Gandolfo). He was already ill: he would die the following year at Castel Gandolfo, on the evening of 6 August 1978 (Vatican City State). Two years after the consecration, on 2 September 1979, John Paul II celebrated Mass here and recalled his predecessor, who in 1977 had come with great joy to celebrate Mass in this church of the lake, which he had wanted built on these shores for the good of residents and tourists alike (Holy See).

The Pope in the boat

On 29 August 2026 the feast reached its high point. Leo XIV celebrated Mass in the church, which belongs to the papal parish of San Tommaso da Villanova, and then joined the procession. Paul VI and John Paul II had taken part, but neither had travelled the stretch on the water (Vatican News). The statue was carried on foot to the Tropicana beach club and then placed aboard the boat “Falco”, decked with yellow and white flowers, together with the Pope, for about 25 minutes of sailing to Giorgio's beach below the Apostolic Palace, escorted by canoes, dragon boats and police vessels (Vatican News).

In his homily Leo XIV described the procession as one of those simple gestures with which we renew our trust in God and our filial devotion to his Most Holy Mother (Holy See). The parish priest, Don Tadeusz Rozmus, recalled Paul VI's intention: a church that “breathes the lake”, because it represents the boat of Saint Peter (Vatican News).

How to visit

The church stands at Via Spiaggia del Lago 12, on the road that runs along the shore (Italia.it). You can reach it on foot from the old town or by car along the lake road. For Mass times, ask at the parish of San Tommaso da Villanova; outside services, the forecourt open onto the water is reason enough to stop, especially at sunset.
