January on the lake — the crater's silence.

November 20265 minute readEditorial team

Once the holidays are over, Castel Gandolfo goes back to being a town of eight thousand people looking down into a crater. January is the coldest month of the year, but also the one in which the village and the lake can be enjoyed most calmly. Here is what to expect, with the figures, and how to spend a winter day on the rim of Lake Albano.

How cold it really gets

Castel Gandolfo lies in climate zone D, with 1,966 degree-days; winter is described as mild and rainy, without excessively low temperatures, and snow is rare, arriving only with widespread cold spells (Wikipedia). There is no long climate series for the village itself: the nearest reference station, Rome Ciampino, at about 130 metres, records a January average of about 7 degrees, with average lows around 3 and an average of 6 to 7 frost days in 1971-2000 (Wikipedia). The historic centre, at 426 metres (Wikipedia), is usually a few degrees cooler, as we explained in our piece on the crater microclimate.

In winter the tramontana blows in from the plain of the Roman Campagna, along with the grecale (Wikipedia): these are the winds that clear the air and bring the sharpest days, when from the viewpoint you can make out the sea and the line of Rome. The same winds, however, make the crater rim biting: better one scarf too many than one too few.

The lake in winter

Lake Albano lies at 293 metres, covers about 6 square kilometres, has a shoreline of around ten kilometres and reaches a depth of 168 metres (Wikipedia). In winter the lungolago, the road running along the northern shore, is almost empty: the walk is flat, with the crater's woods rising behind you. Nature lovers can follow the woodland path on the eastern shore, which also passes the remains of a Roman villa; on the water you will often see cormorants, coots, moorhens and mallards (Wikipedia). Motorboats are banned (Wikipedia), so the silence in the title is not a figure of speech.

The Palace and the gardens

The Papal Palace remains open in winter too. The Vatican Museums' calendar for January 2027 gives admission from 9 am to 4 pm and closing at 5:30 pm, with visitors asked to start leaving the rooms half an hour earlier; at weekends in January and February the Garden of the Moor and the Secret Garden are also open until 4 pm (Vatican Museums). From the Garden of the Moor you reach the gardens of Villa Cybo, whose story we have told. Always check the latest calendar before setting off: closing days change from month to month.

January dates

On 6 January the Befana for children closes the holidays; last year it brought the town council's Christmas programme to an end (Comune di Castel Gandolfo). 20 January is the feast day of Saint Sebastian, patron of the town, and the council offices close (Comune di Castel Gandolfo); the real celebrations, however, take place on the first weekend of September (Comune di Castel Gandolfo). The town statute explains why: the move goes back to a dispensation granted by Pius VII to the townspeople after they were spared by cholera (Town statute).

The same statute provides for the Peace March to be held preferably in January (Town statute), but in recent years the procession has been tied to the anniversary of 10 February 1944 and takes place in February, from Piazza della Libertà through Borgo Laudato si' to the Propaganda Fide College (Comune di Castel Gandolfo).

Practical tips

Days are short: arrive in the morning, when the low light sets the lake's surface glowing, and keep the afternoon for the village. On tramontana days a climb up Monte Cavo gives the widest view over the Castelli; in the evening, the fraschette of Ariccia are the warmest refuge.
